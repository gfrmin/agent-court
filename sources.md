# Sources — what each is, what the book takes from it, and where

*The author session, 3 November 2026. One entry per work the book cites. Each gives the reference, its epistemic status as the book uses it (theorem; impossibility result; necessity theorem under conditions; design principle with a track record; empirical regularity; construction; record), the exact claim the book takes from it, and the chapters that rely on it. Entries are added as chapters are drafted; the charter's §13 is the seed. Where the book's use of a source is narrower than the source's own claim, the entry says so.*

## Decision theory and the owner's utility (for chapter 0)

- **Cox, R. T. (1946).** Probability, frequency and reasonable expectation. *American Journal of Physics* 14(1), 1–13. — *Theorem* (under its regularity conditions). Degrees of belief that are consistent must be probabilities. Taken: "optimally" has one coherent meaning for beliefs. Ch. 0.
- **Savage, L. J. (1954).** *The Foundations of Statistics.* Wiley. — *Theorem* (representation). Coherent preference under uncertainty is expected utility under a subjective probability. Taken: the utility half of "optimally". Ch. 0.
- **de Finetti, B. (1974).** *Theory of Probability*, vol. 1. Wiley. — *Theorem* (coherence; Dutch book). Taken with Cox: conditioning as the unique update; previsions first, as credence had it. Ch. 14.
- **Hadfield-Menell, D., Dragan, A., Abbeel, P. & Russell, S. (2016).** Cooperative inverse reinforcement learning. *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems* 29. — *Framework.* The agent's utility is the human's, unknown, inferred from behaviour. Taken: the alignment commitment — the owner of the utility is the one unscored party. Ch. 0.

## Computation and self-reference

- **Rice, H. G. (1953).** Classes of recursively enumerable sets and their decision problems. *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* 74(2), 358–366. — *Theorem.* Every non-trivial semantic (extensional) property of programs in a Turing-complete language is undecidable. Taken: merit cannot be settled by inspecting the change; statutes must be decidable properties. Not taken: anything about restricted languages, where properties may be decidable. Chs. 0, 2, 10, 11, 16, app. A.
- **Gödel, K. (1931).** Über formal unentscheidbare Sätze der Principia Mathematica und verwandter Systeme I. *Monatshefte für Mathematik und Physik* 38, 173–198. — *Theorem* (second incompleteness). A consistent recursively axiomatised theory interpreting enough arithmetic cannot prove its own consistency. Taken: the court cannot prove its own soundness; it keeps a book instead. Chs. 10, 12, app. A.
- **Löb, M. H. (1955).** Solution of a problem of Leon Henkin. *Journal of Symbolic Logic* 20(2), 115–118. — *Theorem.* If *T* ⊢ Prov(⌜φ⌝) → φ then *T* ⊢ φ. Taken: a sound agent cannot license a successor whose evaluation rule it wrote; the evaluation rule is a fixed point. Chs. 0, 10, 12, 16, app. A.
- **Yudkowsky, E. & Herreshoff, M. (2013).** Tiling agents for self-modifying AI, and the Löbian obstacle. Machine Intelligence Research Institute, technical report. — *Design rule drawn from a theorem.* Trust decreases strictly down a chain of proof-based successors. Taken as stated; the book does not claim jack escapes it, only that it obeys it by keys. Ch. 10, app. A.
- **Kalai, E. & Lehrer, E. (1993).** Rational learning leads to Nash equilibrium. *Econometrica* 61(5), 1019–1045. — *Theorem.* Bayesian learners whose priors put positive mass on the truth about one another converge (the grain-of-truth condition). Taken: the condition, and that it is what a self-model cannot satisfy. Ch. 10, app. A.
- **Nachbar, J. H. (1997).** Prediction, optimization, and learning in repeated games. *Econometrica* 65(2), 275–309; and **(2005)**, Beliefs in repeated games, *Econometrica* 73(2), 459–480. — *Impossibility results.* Properties a class of policies needs to admit a grain of truth cannot all be satisfied for agents of one another's kind. Taken: the impossibility half of the grain-of-truth wall. Ch. 10, app. A.
- **Foster, D. P. & Young, H. P. (2001).** On the impossibility of predicting the behavior of rational agents. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* 98(22), 12848–12853. — *Impossibility result.* Taken with Nachbar. Ch. 10, app. A.
- **Leike, J., Taylor, J. & Fallenstein, B. (2016).** A formal solution to the grain of truth problem. *Proceedings of the 32nd Conference on Uncertainty in Artificial Intelligence (UAI)*; arXiv:1609.05058. — *Construction.* A class of policies containing all computable policies and the Bayes-optimal ones, built on reflective oracles; limit computable, not computable (verified against the text, 3 Nov). Taken: the resolution, and its price. Ch. 10, app. A.
- **Schmidhuber, J. (2007).** Gödel machines: fully self-referential optimal universal self-improvers. In B. Goertzel & C. Pennachin (eds), *Artificial General Intelligence*, Springer, 199–226. — *Construction.* A self-modifier that rewrites itself only on a proof that the rewrite raises expected utility. Taken: as the boundary case, optimal and inert for the reasons Rice and Löb give. Ch. 10, app. A.
- **Pearl, J. (2009).** *Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference*, 2nd ed. Cambridge University Press. — *Theorems of identifiability.* Effects of interventions are not in general identifiable from observational data without assumptions. Taken, with a hedge: an autonomous agent must experiment, and the experiments must be scored by something it does not control. Ch. 2, app. A.
- **Wald, A. (1950).** *Statistical Decision Functions.* Wiley. — *Theorem* (complete class). Under regularity conditions every admissible decision rule is Bayes or a limit of Bayes rules. Taken: the narrow optimality claim — the decision procedure forced on every party inside the court is not dominated. Chs. 0, 16, app. A.

## Forecasting and scoring

- **Dawid, A. P. (1984).** Statistical theory: the prequential approach. *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society A* 147(2), 278–292. doi:10.2307/2981683. — *Principle.* A forecaster is assessed on nothing but its issued forecasts and the realised outcomes. Taken as the file format of the ledger. Verified against the text, 3 Nov: the principle at §5.1, p. 281, offered as a desideratum by analogy with the likelihood principle; the game reading is Shafer and Vovk's, not Dawid's. Chs. 0, 2, 12, app. A.
- **Brier, G. W. (1950).** Verification of forecasts expressed in terms of probability. *Monthly Weather Review* 78(1), 1–3. — *Construction.* The quadratic score. Taken: as the first proper score; not used in jack. Ch. 2, app. A.
- **Good, I. J. (1952).** Rational decisions. *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society B* 14(1), 107–114. — *Construction.* The logarithmic score, and the argument that a fair fee rewards belief. Taken: the score jack uses, and the repository's namesake. Ch. 2, app. A.
- **Savage, L. J. (1971).** Elicitation of personal probabilities and expectations. *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 66(336), 783–801. — *Theorem.* Characterisation of proper scoring rules for probabilities. Taken: propriety as the condition that truthful reporting is optimal. Ch. 2, app. A.
- **Gneiting, T. & Raftery, A. E. (2007).** Strictly proper scoring rules, prediction, and estimation. *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 102(477), 359–378. — *Theorem.* A score is (strictly) proper if and only if it is a subtangent of a (strictly) convex function (Theorem 2.1; subgradient in the categorical case, Theorem 3.2); the log score, "proposed by Good (1952)", is strictly proper. Taken: the definition and the log score's status; the ln *K* mixture argument is the book's, not theirs. Verified against the technical-report version, 3 Nov. Ch. 2, app. A.
- **Blackwell, D. (1951).** Comparison of experiments. *Proceedings of the Second Berkeley Symposium on Mathematical Statistics and Probability*, 93–102. — *Theorem.* An experiment that is a garbling of another is worth less under every utility. Taken lightly: the ordering under which waiting for information has value, and the reason the base rate is the null. Ch. 7, app. A.

## Testing by betting

- **Ville, J. (1939).** *Étude critique de la notion de collectif.* Gauthier-Villars. — *Theorem* (maximal inequality). For a non-negative supermartingale with *E*₀ = 1, P(supₜ *E*ₜ ≥ *c*) ≤ 1/*c*. Taken: 20 is α = 0.05 at any stopping time. Chs. 6, 12, app. A, front matter.
- **Vovk, V. (1990).** Aggregating strategies. *Proceedings of the Third Annual Workshop on Computational Learning Theory*, 371–386. doi:10.5555/92571.92672. — *Theorem.* For mixable losses an aggregating algorithm attains a constant regret bound of the form *c* ln *K*. Taken: that the ln *K* bound is not peculiar to the log score, so the log score is chosen by the bound together with conditioning, not by the bound alone. The original is not open; the bound (ln *N*)/η for η-mixable losses, and the Brier score's mixability at η = 1, were checked against van Erven, Reid and Williamson, "Mixability is Bayes risk curvature relative to log loss", *JMLR* 13 (2012), §§1.1, 5 (which credits the Brier result to Vovk and Zhdanov 2009), 3 Nov. A first correction said other losses need "another learning rate"; for Brier that was wrong. Ch. 2, app. A.
- **Shafer, G. & Vovk, V. (2019).** *Game-Theoretic Foundations for Probability and Finance.* Wiley. doi:10.1002/9781118548035. — *Framework.* A test is a betting strategy; wealth is evidence. Taken as the reading of every promotion statistic. Verified against chs. 1–2 and Exercises 2.10 and 8.8, 3 Nov: capital, testing by betting, 1/α as evidence; Ville's inequality appears there as a bound on upper probability. Ch. 6, app. A.
- **Ramdas, A., Grünwald, P., Vovk, V. & Shafer, G. (2023).** Game-theoretic statistics and safe anytime-valid inference. *Statistical Science* 38(4), 576–601; arXiv:2210.01948. — *Theory.* Test (super)martingales, e-processes, Ville's inequality and optional stopping as the basis of safe anytime-valid inference (the paper's own key words). Taken: the general form the charter's A4.3–4 instantiate. Ch. 6, app. A.
- **Cover, T. M. (1991).** Universal portfolios. *Mathematical Finance* 1(1), 1–29. doi:10.1111/j.1467-9965.1991.tb00002.x. — *Theorem.* The universal portfolio, a mixture over constant-rebalanced portfolios, matches the best one in hindsight in exponential growth rate for arbitrary bounded sequences (§1, eq. 1.7); a polynomial factor holds under regularity conditions, among them that the optimum lies in the interior (Theorem 6.1). Taken: the form a stake chosen by mixture rather than by plug-in would take, and the source of the guarantee. Verified against the text, 3 Nov. Chs. 12, 15.
- **Waudby-Smith, I. & Ramdas, A. (2024).** Estimating means of bounded random variables by betting. *Journal of the Royal Statistical Society Series B* 86(1), 1–27. doi:10.1093/jrsssb/qkad009. — *Construction.* Confidence sequences for bounded means from betting, with Ville's inequality. The strategies compared include predictable plug-ins (§3.1; the authors' earlier name for the approach was "predictable mixture", §2.1) and Kelly-type and diversified bets (aGRAPA, dKelly, §4.4). No regret bound against the best constant bet is the paper's own; regret appears in its history of Cover and Krichevsky–Trofimov (§6). Taken: the practical candidates for replacing A4.3's plug-in stake. Checked against arXiv:2010.09686v7, 3 Nov. Chs. 12, 15.
- **Bahadur, R. R. & Savage, L. J. (1956).** The nonexistence of certain statistical procedures in nonparametric problems. *Annals of Mathematical Statistics* 27(4), 1115–1122. — *Theorem.* Under three conditions on a family (finite means; every real number some member's mean; convexity, §2), which the family of all distributions with bounded but unknown support also meets, no test of the mean has power exceeding its size. Taken: test only outcomes whose range is fixed in advance; report the rest. Verified against the text, 3 Nov. Chs. 5, 6, app. A.
- **Alpern, B. & Schneider, F. B. (1985).** Defining liveness. *Information Processing Letters* 21(4), 181–185. — *Theorem.* Every property is the intersection of a safety and a liveness property; safety is refutable by a finite prefix, liveness never. Taken: replay for safety, writs and a statistic for liveness. Verified against the text, 3 Nov (Theorem 1; the informal distinction credited to Lamport 1977). Ch. 5, app. A.
- **Lamport, L. (1977).** Proving the correctness of multiprocess programs. *IEEE Transactions on Software Engineering* SE-3(2), 125–143. — *Definitions.* Safety and liveness named. Taken: the distinction. Ch. 5, app. A.

## Control and systems

- **Conant, R. C. & Ashby, W. R. (1970).** Every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system. *International Journal of Systems Science* 1(2), 89–97. — *Necessity theorem under conditions.* "The simplest optimal regulator *R* of a reguland *S* produces events *R* which are related to the events *S* by a mapping *h* : *S* → *R*" (§4), with *Z* = ψ(*S*, *R*), entropy of *Z* minimised, the distribution over *S* fixed; optimal non-models exist but are "unnecessarily complex". Taken in that narrow form. Verified against the text, 3 Nov; the first draft wrote ψ(*D*, *R*) and called extra inputs "wrong", which the paper does not. Chs. 0, 1, 16, app. A.
- **Ashby, W. R. (1956).** *An Introduction to Cybernetics.* Chapman & Hall. — *Theorem* (requisite variety, ch. 11). *V*(*Z*) ≥ *V*(*D*) − *V*(*R*). Taken: several proposers of several families; no vendor in law. Verified against the text, 3 Nov: the law at S.11/7 (p. 206), under S.11/5's condition, relaxed in S.11/9; the channel-capacity form at S.11/11. Chs. 0, 1, app. A.
- **Beer, S. (1972).** *Brain of the Firm.* Allen Lane. — *Model.* The Viable System Model. Taken: the nearest institutional ancestor of the five roles; cited in the founding conversation and the essay, not the charter. Ch. 0.
- **Burns, B., Grant, B., Oppenheimer, D., Brewer, E. & Wilkes, J. (2016).** Borg, Omega, and Kubernetes. *ACM Queue* 14(1). — *Engineering principle.* Level-triggered reconciliation loses nothing on a missed event. Taken: the clerk reconciles state; it does not handle events. Chs. 1, 5, 11, app. A.
- **Schneider, F. B. (1990).** Implementing fault-tolerant services using the state machine approach: a tutorial. *ACM Computing Surveys* 22(4), 299–319. — *Construction.* A deterministic fold over an ordered log is a replicated state machine. Taken: fold(law, log). Ch. 1, app. A.
- **Lamport, L. (1978).** Time, clocks, and the ordering of events in a distributed system. *Communications of the ACM* 21(7), 558–565. — *Theorem and construction.* Order from causality, not clocks. Taken: the chain orders the record; the kernel never reads a clock. Chs. 1, 3, app. A.

## Law

- **Hart, H. L. A. (1961).** *The Concept of Law.* Clarendon Press. (Ch. V.) — *Design principle by elimination.* Primary rules plus recognition, change and adjudication; a regime lacking one cannot answer one of three questions. Taken: C0 is exactly the three. Verified against the third edition (2012), 3 Nov: the three defects and their remedies (ch. V §3); the three together are sufficient for "indisputably a legal system", and Hart does not claim them necessary — the defect-for-defect reading is the book's. Chs. 3, 4, app. A.
- **Fuller, L. L. (1964).** *The Morality of Law.* Yale University Press. (Ch. II.) doi:10.12987/9780300191653 (revised edition, 1969). — *Design principle with a catalogue of failures.* Eight ways to fail to make law. Taken: eight checks; congruence as the one prompt-based harnesses fail. Verified against the revised edition, 3 Nov: the eight in Fuller's order (ch. II, pp. 38–39); a *total* failure in any one yields no legal system; congruence failures make obedience futile. Chs. 4, 8, 14, app. A.
- **Coke, E. (1610).** Dr Bonham's Case, 8 Co. Rep. 107a. — *Maxim.* *Nemo iudex in causa sua.* Taken: `self_judged`, by principal. Ch. 4, app. A.
- **Habeas Corpus Act 1679** (31 Cha. 2 c. 2). — *Institution.* A writ derived from custody and the clock that no custodian may let lapse. Taken: the clerk's writs. Ch. 3, 7.
- **Blackstone, W. (1765–69).** *Commentaries on the Laws of England.* Clarendon Press. — *Doctrine.* Precedent. Taken: a rule enters from a witnessed case. Ch. 4.
- **Jefferson, T. (1789).** Letter to James Madison, 6 September. — *Maxim.* The earth belongs to the living. Taken: windows are maxima; law lapses when undefended. Ch. 9.
- **Madison, J. (1788).** *The Federalist* No. 51. — *Maxim.* Enable the government to control the governed; then oblige it to control itself. Taken: the build order C0–C1 then C2. Chs. 9, 11.
- **Ostrom, E. (1990).** *Governing the Commons.* Cambridge University Press. — *Empirical design principles.* Graduated sanctions; monitors accountable to the monitored; nested enterprises. Taken: the escalation ladder; the sovereign as a role, not an operator. Chs. 12, 15.
- **Hayek, F. A. (1973).** *Law, Legislation and Liberty*, vol. 1: *Rules and Order.* University of Chicago Press. — *Empirical regularity.* Grown order against made order. Taken: rules are found by cases. Chs. 11, 15, app. A.
- **Suber, P. (1990).** *The Paradox of Self-Amendment.* Peter Lang. — *Analysis.* An entrenched rule is first made mutable, as a separate move. Taken: `decision transmute` before `amend` [SPEC §10.13]. Chs. 4, 9.

- **Arrow, K. J. (1951).** *Social Choice and Individual Values.* Wiley. — *Theorem.* No aggregation of several orderings into one satisfies unrestricted domain, Pareto, independence and non-dictatorship. Taken: the one-owner assumption is a limit of the construction, not an extension (ch. 15 §15.6). Ch. 15.

## Decision under uncertainty, measurement and growth

- **Arrow, K. J. & Fisher, A. C. (1974).** Environmental preservation, uncertainty, and irreversibility. *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 88(2), 312–319. doi:10.2307/1883074. — *Theorem.* Quasi-option value of waiting before an irreversible act. Taken: gates only before the irreversible. Verified against the text, 3 Nov: "quasi-option value" is their term (p. 315); the result is in a two-period model with risk neutrality; they price irreversibility rather than forbid it (p. 319), so the converse and the gate are the book's. Ch. 9, app. A.
- **Henry, C. (1974).** Investment decisions under uncertainty: the "irreversibility effect". *American Economic Review* 64(6), 1006–1012. — *Theorem.* The same result independently. Taken: as corroboration. Ch. 9, app. A.
- **Dixit, A. K. & Pindyck, R. S. (1994).** *Investment under Uncertainty.* Princeton University Press. — *Textbook.* Taken: the general treatment. App. A.
- **Goodhart, C. A. E. (1975).** Problems of monetary management: the U.K. experience. *Papers in Monetary Economics*, vol. I, Reserve Bank of Australia. — *Empirical regularity.* A regularity collapses under control pressure. Taken: proper scores on outcomes the party does not control; procedure counted against object. Chs. 0, 2, 10, app. A.
- **Campbell, D. T. (1979).** Assessing the impact of planned social change. *Evaluation and Program Planning* 2(1), 67–90. — *Empirical regularity.* The same for social indicators. Taken: as corroboration. App. A.
- **Manheim, D. & Garrabrant, S. (2018).** Categorizing variants of Goodhart's law. arXiv:1803.04585v4. — *Taxonomy.* Four categories — regressional, extremal (model insufficiency; change in regime), causal, adversarial — verified against the text, 3 Nov. Taken: the adversarial case is the court's. Ch. 2, app. A.
- **Gall, J. (1977).** *Systemantics: How Systems Work and Especially How They Fail.* Quadrangle. — *Empirical regularity.* A working complex system evolved from a working simple one. Taken: layers promoted before the next begins; stated as a regularity, never a theorem. Ch. 11, app. A.
