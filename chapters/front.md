# A Court for Agents

## Law, scoring and the self-modifying machine

**Guy Freeman** — with counsel and the author sessions (Claude, `claude-fable-5-1`), whose acts and forecasts are scored in the text like anyone's.

*First draft, November 2026. The monograph of [jack](https://github.com/renavondata/jack), a court for a self-modifying agent, built one inevitable step at a time. The book is written as a construction: each chapter starts from what the last one built, names the problem it cannot solve, states the result that leaves no choice, and builds the smallest thing that discharges it — in code, cited to the line; in law, cited to the clause; on the record, cited to the entry. Every number carries a tag saying where it came from and how it was recomputed, and a reader with a clone of jack can repeat it.*

*The source of this book is the public repository [gfrmin/agent-court](https://github.com/gfrmin/agent-court). Findings that would change a court document are raised there as issues; essays for [gfrm.in](https://gfrm.in) arrive as pull requests. The founding essay is [The Science Was Done. The Engineering Wasn't.](https://gfrm.in/posts/the-science-was-done/) (1 November 2026).*

### How to read it

Statisticians may start at chapter 6, where the tests are theorems. Readers from AI safety at chapters 2 and 10, the bet and the lock. Institutional economists at chapters 4, 8 and 14, the law and its price. Everyone else at chapter 0, which is the question — and chapter 16 second, which is the referee's case, written first so that the referee can be answered from the record rather than argued with.

### Conventions

`[seq N]` is an entry of jack's `log.jsonl`; `[CHARTER §n]`, `[SPEC §n]`, `[BRIEF-…]` a clause of a court document; `[status.py … @commit]` a tool run the reader can repeat; `[REPORT.md …, as transcript]` what counsel reported, never the source of a number. The "figure" that opens each chapter is a piece of ordinary human life that has the chapter's shape; it illustrates and never evidences. Each chapter ends with what it leaves open and what would show it wrong.
