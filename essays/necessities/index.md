The founding note for [jack](https://github.com/renavondata/jack) argued that nearly everything underneath a self-improving agent was finished decades ago, and that what is new is only the economy. This essay makes the stronger claim the book that follows rests on: that the pieces are not merely available but *forced*. Each component of jack's core is there because a result says that without it the agent cannot be evaluated, tested, governed or stopped. Remove one and you have not simplified the design; you have made an error that a theorem names.

"Necessary" means one thing throughout. A mechanism is necessary if removing it leaves a stated result unmet — the agent inspected where it can only be run, trusted where it can only be bet on, timed where it can only be counted, governed by a rule that cannot guide conduct. The claim is never that jack's mechanism is the only one that could discharge the result. It is that *some* mechanism must, that jack's is one, and that you can find it: in 450 lines of kernel, in a numbered clause of the charter, or at a numbered entry of the log. Everything below gives you the place to look.

I take each result in four parts: what it says, with its hypotheses; what it forbids or forces; the mechanism; and what would show it misapplied. The last part matters most. A book about pre-registered falsifiers should pre-register its own.

## Evaluation: why the change cannot be inspected

**Rice (1953).** Every non-trivial property of what a program *does*, in a language that can write the next version of itself, is undecidable. So "is this change an improvement?" has no procedure that inspects the change; and since improvement is a property of the program in the world, not of the program, it is settled by running the change and scoring the outcome. jack's charter confines its statutes to decidable properties on exactly this ground, and separates validity from merit in the same clause: questions of merit "are forecasts, scored when the world resolves them" (CHARTER §3). Every check the kernel makes is syntactic and terminates — a canonical serialisation, a hash, a signature, a type in a list of powers (SPEC §8). *Misapplied if* anyone proposes that the judge check whether a change is good, or treats a green test run as evidence that the work was worth anything. The predecessor court spent three review rounds on how a status file cited a green run while the code was green throughout; that is recorded as a failure (CHARTER §1, F4).

**Löb (1955), Gödel (1931), and the grain of truth.** A consistent theory cannot prove its own soundness, and an agent that acts on proofs cannot soundly trust a successor whose proof system it wrote: trust strictly decreases down a chain of self-modifiers. A computable prior over agents of its own kind cannot contain a grain of truth, so a Bayesian self-model either puts zero mass on the truth or does not converge. These force one design rule: the evaluation rule is not something the agent improves. In jack, reach is a matter of keys. The trusted base — the kernel, the second checker, the judge, the workflows, the specification — changes only by a `push` entry naming its digest, and the `push` power sits on one key, the sovereign's (log seqs 1 and 33). Builders, reviewers, counsel and the merging herald hold `proposal`, `forecast`, `resolution`, `merge`; none holds `push`, `amend` or `key`. The charter closes the loop on the agent itself: "whatever counsel and builders are forbidden is what the agent of C3 is forbidden, by the same judge, keys and amendment path" (A2.10). *Misapplied if* the book claims more than the rule — that jack "solves" the Löbian obstacle, or that the court is provably sound. It is neither. The court's soundness is bet on, not proved; that is the subject of another essay.

**Dawid (1984).** A forecaster is judged on nothing but the forecasts it actually issued and the outcomes that then occurred. In jack this is a file format: a `forecast` entry carries a probability as an integer from 1 to 999 per mille and a deadline; a `resolution` names it, records 0 or 1, and cites hashed evidence; timestamps may not decrease, so the probability is fixed at its position; a second resolution is refused; a resolution by the forecaster's own principal is refused as `self_judged` (SPEC §§5–8). *Misapplied if* a score ever uses a probability fixed after its event. The record already names the two ways: a forecast timestamped at or after the event's cutoff is listed as `rigged` and not used — there are three on the log, seqs 288–290 — and a later forecast of the same event by the same principal is listed as a `revision`; the first stands.

**Proper scores (Good 1952; Gneiting and Raftery 2007).** Only a strictly proper score makes reporting your belief the unique best move; the logarithmic score is one, and it is the one under which a Bayes mixture over \(K\) forecasters loses at most \(\ln K\) more than the best of them — a telescoping identity that holds for the log score and no other. jack scores log-loss, per mille, against a base-rate forecaster that is Laplace's rule over the resolved prefix of the forecast's type, and its aggregate is the Bayes mixture with the uniform prior and no learning rate: "η = 1 is the telescoping identity, the uniform prior the minimax choice for the ln K bound" (BRIEF-C2a). *Misapplied if* a score that is not strictly proper, or a transformation that breaks propriety, enters a promotion criterion or an election.

**Goodhart (1975).** A measure that becomes a target decays — unless the measured party cannot move it except by doing the thing it measures. Two conditions give that: the score is proper, and the outcomes come from processes the party does not control. A third follows for the court: its own statistics can be manufactured by producing more court, so it counts procedure against object and stops above assize's ratio of 5 : 1 (A3.4). Outcomes in jack come from a results page saved and hashed, or a catalogue's modification time; the resolver is never the forecaster; and the sovereign's own acts are typed by entry type and signing key, "so the split is mechanical and fixed in advance, not judged afterwards" (CHARTER §7). The first window of the ledger ran at 127 procedure entries to 32 about the world; the clerk's window ran at 67 to 82 — the first in which the court wrote more about the world than about itself (`tools/status.py` on the log at those windows' exit prefixes, seqs 188 and 337). *Misapplied if* a promotion test counts something a proposer can produce without doing the work.

## Testing: tests you may look at whenever you like

**Ville (1939); Shafer and Vovk; Ramdas et al.** For a non-negative supermartingale with unit start, the probability that it ever reaches \(c\) is at most \(1/c\) — at every stopping time, including the moment someone decided to look. The operator of this system is one person who looks when he wakes. So every promotion test is a bet whose wealth is the evidence, 20 is a rejection at \(\alpha = 0.05\) whenever it is seen, and the only discipline is that each stake be decided before the outcome it bets on. jack's liveness statistic is \(E_t = \prod_i (1 + \lambda_i (Y_i - p_0))\) with the stake a Laplace function of the prefix and strictly below \(1/p_0\) (A4.3); its value statistic is a bet on a bounded difference of log-losses (A4.4); the wealth is never reset across windows (A4.1); and a window ends at the earlier of its stated end and the sovereign's `promote`, which may be entered only once every criterion holds (A5). The rule has already bitten: the clerk's window carried \(E\) to 214358881/8398080 ≈ 25.5 over nine items in a day and closed there, with almost thirteen of its fourteen days unused (seq 337). *Misapplied if* a stake is set with knowledge of its outcome, the null is estimated from the window it judges, or the wealth is reset — which is \(k\) draws at one bound, and is the defect that forced amendment A4.

**Bahadur and Savage (1956).** In the family of all distributions with a finite mean, no test of the mean has power above its size. The quantities an operator most wants to see fall — minutes, dollars, repairs per merge — are unbounded, and no procedure can certify that their mean is small. So jack tests only bounded outcomes — an item completed without repair, a log-loss difference bounded by \(\ln 999\) — and reports the rest as numbers, never as evidence (CHARTER §6). *Misapplied if* anyone proposes "\(E \ge 20\) on mean latency".

**Alpern and Schneider (1985).** Every property is the intersection of a safety property, refutable by a finite prefix, and a liveness property, which no finite prefix refutes. That nothing invalid was ever admitted is safety: two independent checkers replay every proper prefix of every seeded log — 100,000 logs, 636,392 prefixes, no disagreement in the run of record. That obligations do not rot is liveness: it can only be given a deadline and tested statistically, which is what the clerk's writs and the \(E\)-process above do. *Misapplied if* a report claims liveness from a replay or safety from a statistic.

## Control: what the governor must contain

**Conant and Ashby (1970); Ashby (1956).** An optimal regulator acts on a representation of the regulated system sufficient to determine the outcome, and on nothing outside it; and it needs at least the variety of the disturbances it faces. jack's state is a fold of the law over the log — head, keys, charter, trusted base, forecasts and which are resolved — and the clerk derives every obligation from that state and the clock, which enters only as an argument; the kernel never reads one. The judge's grounds are "stated over powers and paths and never over a party's name" (A2.3), the election rule is "an entrant, not law" (CHARTER §5), and the proposers are sessions of more than one model family. *Misapplied if* an obligation lives outside the record. Assize's stalls were found by a human watching a phone; jack's own four forecasts expired undischarged in the ledger's first window while the clerk was still in counsel's head (seqs 45–47, 56). That is the clerk's forcing witness.

## Law: the smallest thing that can bind

**Hart (1961).** A legal system is primary rules plus exactly three secondary ones — recognition, change, adjudication — and lacks one of three answers without any of them. jack's zeroth layer was cut to exactly those: a valid entry; a `key` entry, an `amend` preceded by a `transmute` and in force from a later height, a `push` naming a digest; a judge that runs trunk's kernel over the proposal's entries as data. The kernel holding all three is 450 lines (`tools/count_kernel.py`). No primary rule has yet been needed, and the charter says that if none is by the end of C2, "the thesis that three secondary rules suffice is on trial" (A2.1). *Misapplied if* a rule of conduct is written into the kernel in advance of a case.

**Fuller (1964).** Eight ways to fail to make law, each a check. Prospectivity is the fold: the keys that verify an entry are those before its own effect, so a key declared at \(i\) does not sign \(i\) and a revocation never reaches back. Possibility of compliance: the kernel refuses a public key no one could be the sole holder of, and a `key` entry that would leave no key able to declare keys. Congruence — the declared rule is the applied rule — is the principle every prompt-based harness fails, because the model that reads the rule is the party it binds; jack's enforcement map records row by row whether a principle is enforced by code or by hand, and the clerk's window required the rows it moved to read `code`. One thing in the founding note went further than the record: the rule that every rule is admitted with a witness that violates it and one that satisfies it is written in the charter and waits for the first primary rule. It is law-in-waiting, not kernel. *Misapplied if* a rule binds an entry that preceded it, or the declared and enforced rules differ.

## Enforcement: code at a seam

**Anderson (1972); Saltzer and Schroeder (1975).** The reference monitor is always invoked, tamper-proof and small enough to verify; authority is a reference held, not a rule obeyed. Every proposal is judged by trunk's checkers in a workflow that comes from trunk; the trusted base is five paths compared by content, so a rename or a symbolic link under them is refused like an edit; a checker that cannot decide — a crash, a timeout — is treated as a refusal; and the herald's key holds `merge` alone, so the process that merges green proposals can do nothing else — every one of the 85 entries it has signed since that key was declared is a `merge` (seq 201 onward). *Misapplied if* the accused supplies its own test. Assize's F1 — a pull request's checks ran the pull request's own harness — is the witness, and `judge_from_proposal` is the pin.

**Thompson (1984); Wheeler (2005).** One checker is evidence of nothing; two independent lineages that agree are evidence of the specification. jack's second checker is in Go, by sessions of another model family that read the specification and the vectors and nothing else; the differential compares the two on every proper prefix and counts silence as disagreement. *Misapplied if* the second checker is written with sight of the first, or the comparison is on whole logs only — a checker wrong only on a prefix would pass, and the pin `whole_log_only` exists for that.

## Irreversibility and growth

**Arrow and Fisher (1974).** An irreversible act carries an extra cost — the value of the information waiting would bring — and a reversible one carries none, so a gate before it buys nothing. Hard gates stand only before the irreversible: the sovereign's verbs are `genesis`, `key`, `amend`, `push`, `decision`, and the last key able to declare keys cannot be revoked. Everything reversible is priced: a refused proposal costs resubmission, a code cap is a budget raised by a push and enters no score, a failed window is followed and never patched. *Misapplied if* a hard gate stands before a reversible act or none before an irreversible one — assize managed both at once, with force-push ungated for a week while bookkeeping on a status file blocked merges (CHARTER §1, F5).

**Gall (1977); Hayek (1973).** A working complex system is invariably found to have evolved from a working simple one. Not a theorem; a base rate, and my own history is seven draws from it. jack is built in layers, each promoted at a crossing before the next begins, with rules of conduct entering only from cases. The cost is serial windows, and it is paid: one proposal, to prepare the agent layer before the layers below had crossed, was withdrawn on exactly this ground.

## What this adds up to

The law is minimal in a definite sense: it contains only mechanisms that discharge one of the results above, and constants that carry a proof or were pre-stated before the window they bound — \(\ln K\), \(1/\alpha = 20\), stakes derived from the prefix, a grid, a cap, a null committed in advance. Everything else — every model, every prior finer than uniform, every base rate finer than a type, every threshold, every model of what I would say — is an entrant that stands for election and can lose. The test of minimality is mechanical: a mechanism in the law with no row above is an entrant installed as law; two mechanisms for one row is one too many.

What it does not add up to is sufficiency, and the book does not claim it except by construction of its falsifiers, which the charter pre-stated: the kernel outgrowing what a person can read; a liveness window failing against the predecessor's baseline; repairs falling only because acts were re-typed; procedure outgrowing object. And the one sense in which it is optimal is Wald's and narrow: every discretionary choice the court makes about itself is a bet with a stated probability, scored, so its decision procedure cannot be dominated. A court that chose otherwise would be.

**What would show this wrong.** A "misapplied if" clause firing on the record. A result overstated — Conant–Ashby read as more than it proves, Löb read as a theorem about jack rather than a rule it obeys; the hypotheses are stated so a reader can check. A necessity with two mechanisms, or a mechanism with no necessity. And the one that would cut deepest: a court built without one of these rows that has run longer than jack, at a lower price to its operator, on a world that charges. I know of none, and the predecessors are seven attempts to build one.
