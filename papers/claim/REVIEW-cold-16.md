## The report (verbatim)

## 1. The one idea

An AI agent that can modify its own code cannot be trusted to evaluate itself, so the governing institution that judges it should be built outside the agent's reach before the agent exists and exercised first on the model sessions that build it. Rather than trusting that institution as fixed infrastructure, the institution should keep a signed, append-only book on its own operation and gain powers layer by layer only when valid sequential tests on that record reject weak liveness or predictive value—tests that can also expose when the institution's apparent evidence of value is an artifact of batch resolution or a weak baseline.

**Does the abstract state it, and does the body deliver it?** Yes to both. The abstract (line 14) states the problem, the institutional proposal, the six propositions with their Lean-checked statistical core and the block-form correction to per-event betting, the retrospective audit showing that the value evidence survives against a type-pooled base rate but vanishes against a per-dataset base rate, and the explicit scope disclaimer. The body delivers the setting and motivating literature (§§2–3), the formal guarantees and filtration counterexample (§4), the build-order argument (§5), the retrospective recomputation and falsifier audit on `renavondata/jack` (§6), the related-work positioning (§7), and the limitations (§8).

- **Verification recipes embedded in prose (§5.2, line 283; §6, line 325):** The bracketed regex/prefix recipe for counting proposal titles on line 283 and the half-open interval scan notes on line 325 belong in `04-selfcheck.md` or a footnote rather than the main text.

## 2. Scope

**Accuracy and prominence of the scope statement.** The scope disclaimer is accurate, candid, and impossible to miss. The final sentence of the Abstract (line 14) states: *"The paper offers a design and an audit, not an evaluation: the implementation has not yet tied its powers to these tests, and no self-modifying agent has yet been built under the design."* The same boundary is restated in Contribution 3 (line 29), the scope paragraph of §1 (line 31), §4.2 (line 129), §5.1 (line 268), Table 2 (line 301), §8 (lines 361, 377, 379), and the Conclusion (line 385). No careful reader can mistake the paper for an empirical evaluation of a self-modifying agent or a validation of test-conditioned capability grants.

**Soundness and utility as an arXiv preprint.** So scoped, this is a sound and genuinely useful `cs.AI` preprint. First, its institutional architecture—law as a prefix fold determining entry admissibility, claims of merit priced as proper-scored forecasts, sequential testing of principal repair burden alongside predictive value with wealth carried across failed windows, and replay-bound evaluator upgrades—addresses a real gap in recursive self-improvement governance. Second, §4.3's analysis of why per-event sequential betting loses its Type I error control under batched resolution (via a filtration mismatch between the forecast-cutoff null and resolution-order conditioning), paired with the lag-classed block test, Lean proofs, and `sim_filtration.py`, is a clean methodological contribution applicable to any sequential agent benchmark whose tasks resolve in batches. Third, the retrospective audit of the 7,810-entry public record in §6 is a model of negative-result transparency: it shows how a 56-order-of-magnitude apparent e-value ($1.52 \times 10^{60}$) drops to $\sim 10^4$ under lag-classed block betting and vanishes completely ($\max W = 1$) once the baseline is conditioned on dataset identity.

**What a peer-reviewed venue would also need:**
1. **Closing the authority loop in code and reaching C3:** Implementing promotion-gated key/capability grants (closing the gap in §5.1) and running an actual self-modifying agent (C3) under the institution.
2. **Controlled experimental comparison (§8, line 377):** Comparing the full architecture against simpler governance baselines (e.g., a fixed evaluator with per-edit gates such as PACE, SGM, or SEA) across repeated runs and matched tasks, measuring both agent utility and human repair burden.
3. **Adversarial evaluation:** Red-teaming the institution with strategic agent policies attempting reward hacking, ratio padding, performative outcome manipulation, or collusion, rather than routine hourly forecasts by `gpt-5.4-mini`.
4. **A complete liveness batch unit and skill-discriminating value baseline:** Fixing the batch-vs-expiry unit left open in §4.3 (line 194) and testing prospectively against a per-dataset or incumbent baseline.

## 3. Correctness

All six propositions, their proofs, and the Remark and Example in §4.3 are mathematically sound under their stated hypotheses.

- **Proposition 1 (prospectivity, lines 113–121):**
  - *(a)* Valid. Because $S_i$ is a pure fold of $e_0, \dots, e_{i-1}$ and $V(S_i, e_i)$ depends only on $e_0, \dots, e_i$ with the first refusal determining the verdict (`SPEC §1`, `kernel/check.py:103–111`), any extension $L' = L \Vert M$ preserves the verdict on every entry of $L$.
  - *(b)* Valid. Requiring both $L$ and $L^* \neq L$ to be linked and numbered by position from $0$ (`seq` $= i$) closes the proper-suffix counterexample. Walking backward from the last lines (which both hash to $h$), either two differing lines share a hash at some step, or one log is shorter and its genesis line (`seq` $= 0$) is paired with a line of positive `seq` in the longer log while sharing the `parent` hash of the identical line after them (in fact, when $|L| \neq |L^*|$, their terminal lines already differ in `seq` while sharing hash $h$).
- **Proposition 2 (propriety; no self-judgement, lines 131–137):**
  - *(a)* Valid. For $\pi \in (0,1)$, $g(p) = \pi\ln p + (1-\pi)\ln(1-p)$ has $g'(p) = (\pi - p)/(p(1-p))$ and $g''(p) = -\pi/p^2 - (1-\pi)/(1-p)^2 < 0$ on $(0,1)$, so $p = \pi$ is the unique continuous maximiser. By strict concavity on $(0,1)$, $g$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\pi]$ and strictly decreasing on $[\pi,1)$, so its maximum on $\{1,\dots,999\}/1000$ lies at one (or two tied) grid points adjacent to $\pi$, or at $0.001$ or $0.999$ when $\pi \notin [0.001, 0.999]$.
  - *(b)* Valid by construction of `SPEC §8` step 8a (`self_judged`, `kernel/check.py:82–83`), which compares the resolution's `proposer` with the forecast's recorded `proposer` after step 8 verifies that `proposer` matches the signing key's registered `principal`.
- **Proposition 3 (value, lines 153–183):**
  - *Setup and bounds:* Under assumption (A)—now explicitly stating on line 153 that when $B_m$'s forecasts close, every outcome of earlier blocks is realised and none of $B_m$'s own is—the weights $w_{k,m}$, forecasts $p_{k,i}$, base rates $b_i$, and stake $\lambda_m \in [0, 1 - \frac{1}{m+1}] \subset [0,1)$ are all $\mathcal{G}_{m-1}$-measurable. On the resolved subset $R_m \subseteq B_m$ (replacing $B_m$ throughout, with unit factor when $R_m = \varnothing$), clipping $p_{k,i}, b_i \in [10^{-3}, 1-10^{-3}]$ bounds $\ln(p_{k,i}(Y_i)/b_i(Y_i)) \in [-\ln 999, \ln 999]$, so each member's product likelihood ratio on $R_m$ and their convex combination $Q_m(Y_{R_m})/\prod_{i \in R_m} b_i(Y_i)$ lie in $[999^{-|R_m|}, 999^{|R_m|}]$, giving $\bar{D}_m \in [-1, 1]$.
  - *Sufficient condition (line 173):* Conditioning on both $\mathcal{G}_{m-1}$ and which events resolve ($R_m$) makes $|R_m|$ non-random inside the conditional expectation, so non-positive conditional expected joint log-score advantage given $(\mathcal{G}_{m-1}, R_m)$ implies $\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_m \mid \mathcal{G}_{m-1}] \le 0$ by the tower property.
  - *(a)* Since $\lambda_m \in [0,1)$ and $|\bar{D}_m| \le 1$, $1 + \lambda_m \bar{D}_m > 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[1 + \lambda_m \bar{D}_m \mid \mathcal{G}_{m-1}] = 1 + \lambda_m \mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_m \mid \mathcal{G}_{m-1}] \le 1$. Thus $W_t$ is a non-negative supermartingale with $W_0 = 1$, and Ville's inequality gives $\Pr(\sup_t W_t \ge 1/\alpha) \le \alpha$. For data-dependently created lag classes $\ell = 1, 2, \dots$ assigned predictably at cutoff, the thresholds $\ell(\ell+1)/\alpha$ give $\sum_{\ell \ge 1} \alpha/(\ell(\ell+1)) = \alpha$ by the union bound.
  - *(b)* For a single sequence of blocks where $w_{k,m}$ is the Bayes posterior updated from the scored blocks $j < m$, $\prod_{m \le t} Q_m(Y_{B_m}) = K^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^K \prod_{m \le t}\prod_{i \in B_m} p_{k,i}(Y_i) \ge K^{-1}\max_k \prod_{m \le t}\prod_{i \in B_m} p_{k,i}(Y_i)$. Taking logs and subtracting $\sum_{m \le t}\sum_{i \in B_m}\ln b_i(Y_i)$ yields both inequalities since the base-rate forecaster is in the roster.
- **Remark and Example in §4.3 (lines 186–188):**
  - *Remark:* Sound. It identifies the defect of per-event betting in resolution order as a filtration mismatch between the forecast-cutoff filtration $\mathcal{G}_{m-1}$ and the resolution-order filtration, notes that freezing stakes within a sitting does not make a product of correlated factors a supermartingale, and notes both costs of the block form: reduced power under conditional independence, and the fact that a Bayes mixture of product forecasts can beat the base rate's product forecast under the joint log score simply by representing within-sitting outcome correlation (whereas replacing $Q_m$ by the product of marginals $\prod_i q_i$ breaks Proposition 3(b)'s $\ln K$ regret bound).
  - *Example:* Sound and verified. With shared outcome $Y \sim \mathrm{Bern}(1/2)$, base rate $b = 1/2$, and randomised aggregate forecast $q \in \{0.95, 0.05\}$ independent of $Y$, the conditional drift is $\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_m \mid \mathcal{G}_{m-1}] = \frac{\ln(1.9) + \ln(0.1)}{2\ln 999} \approx -0.1202$. Running `sim_filtration.py` reproduces every figure and 95% interval: $0/4{,}000$ crossings for the block form (rule-of-three 95% upper bound $3/4000 = 0.075\%$); $530/4{,}000 = 13.25\%$ ($13.3\%$, Wald 95% CI $12.2\text{--}14.3\%$) for per-event betting at $n=20$, 300 sittings; $1{,}007/2{,}000 = 50.35\%$ ($50.4\%$, Wald 95% CI $48.2\text{--}52.5\%$) at $n=50$, 200 sittings; and $0\%$ when outcomes within a sitting are independent. Running `sim_filtration.py --roster 20 300 2000` gives $0.2850$ (implementation form) vs. $0.0000$ (block form), and the script's docstring rightly notes that `--roster` is not a true null for the joint score because the mixture of constant $0.95$ and $0.05$ members models the shared outcome ($\mathbb{E}[\bar{D}_1] \approx +0.085 > 0$).
- **Proposition 4 (liveness, lines 196–204):**
  - *(a)* Valid. Since $\hat{q}_j = (s_j+1)/(n_j+2) \in (0,1)$ and $p_0 \in (0,1)$, $\lambda_j = \max(0, (\hat{q}_j - p_0)/(p_0(1-p_0))) \in [0, 1/p_0)$, so for $Y_j \in [0,1]$ (or batch average $\bar{Y}_j \in [0,1]$), $1 + \lambda_j(Y_j - p_0) \ge 1 - \lambda_j p_0 > 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[1 + \lambda_j(Y_j - p_0) \mid \mathcal{F}_{j-1}] \le 1$. Continuing one product across windows makes every window end a stopping time of a single supermartingale, so Ville's inequality applies once at level $\alpha$.
  - *(b)* Valid. With false-crossing probabilities $r_j \le \alpha$, independence gives $1 - \prod_{j=1}^k(1 - r_j) \le 1 - (1-\alpha)^k$ (attained when each $r_j = \alpha$), and the union bound gives $\sum_{j=1}^k r_j \le k\alpha$.
- **Proposition 5 (conjunctive promotion, lines 210–212):** Valid application of the intersection–union principle: $\{\text{promote}\} \subseteq \{\sup_t W_t \ge 1/\alpha\} \cap \{\sup_t M_t \ge 1/\alpha\}$, so under $H_0^{\mathrm{val}} \cup H_0^{\mathrm{live}}$ its probability is bounded by whichever component null holds.
- **Proposition 6 (replay-bound change, lines 226–237):** Valid structural statement of what the replay admission condition guarantees on $R^+$ (part (a)), what item 15's historical evaluator dispatch guarantees after the installing push (part (b)), and what remains uncertified outside $R^+$ or under different auxiliary inputs (part (c)).

**Validity of the method in `block_on_log.py`.** As a fixed protocol for Proposition 3(a)'s resolved-subset, cutoff-filtration null, the method in `block_on_log.py` is now genuinely adapted:
1. *Pre-cutoff sitting membership and deadline:* Lines 91–94 include an event in sitting `c` only if a forecast with `e["ts"] < c` exists, fixing its deadline as the maximum `resolves_by` among pre-cutoff forecasts.
2. *Strict deadline expiry:* Lines 125–128 treat any resolution with `ts > deadline` as expired at `deadline` (`Y = None`, `done = deadline`), so every member event's completion timestamp `done <= deadline` is a stopping time and no post-deadline resolution can retroactively alter a completed sitting.
3. *Predictable lag-class assignment:* Lines 138–145 assign every sitting (including pending ones) at its cutoff `c` to the first existing class `i` with `last_done[i] < c`. Because `done` is a stopping time, whether `last_done[i] < c` holds is completely observable at time `c`, and within each class assumption (A) holds strictly.
4. *Cutoff-measurable weights, base rates, and ablation differences:* Mixture weights use only timely resolved member events with `done < c` (lines 162–165), base rates use only resolutions with `ts < c` (line 117, `bisect_left`), and the per-event ablation stamps all per-event differences of sitting `c` with `max(e["done"] for e in sittings[c])` (line 185), so an event enters `prior` at a later cutoff only once its *whole sitting* is complete. As line 309 notes, cross-class weight updates preserve predictability for Proposition 3(a) while forgoing Proposition 3(b)'s telescoping regret bound.
