## The report (verbatim)

## 1. The one idea

**The message in two sentences.** A self-modifying AI agent can be trusted only as far as it is governed by an institution outside its reach that is itself tested rather than trusted—built before the agent, imposed first on the model sessions that build it, and granted powers layer by layer only through valid sequential tests on its own signed record. Those sequential tests must themselves be statistically valid under batch resolution and honest baselines, so that the institution's book can expose when its apparent evidence of value is an artifact of per-event compounding or a weak comparator.

**Does the abstract state it, and does the body deliver it?** Yes to both. The four-sentence abstract states the problem (trusted evaluators and the limits from Rice, Löb and Goodhart), the institutional construction (law as a fold over a signed log, claims as log-scored forecasts, conjunctive promotion on value and repair-counted liveness before the agent exists), the formal and methodological contribution (six propositions with a Lean-checked statistical core and the block-form correction to per-event betting), and the empirical payoff (retrospective recomputation on a public record showing that the value evidence survives against a type-pooled base rate but vanishes against a per-dataset base rate). The body delivers each part coherently, with §6 moved ahead of Related Work (§7) so the evidence follows the construction directly.

**What in the body does not serve it.**
1. **The Draft 9 preamble (lines 6–11):** Internal session metadata, runner issue numbers, and references to private paths (`claude/claim/03-review-round1.md`, `04-selfcheck.md`, `05-what-the-paper-is-about.md`) do not belong in a submission manuscript.

### Proposition 2 (propriety; no self-judgement, lines 161–168)
- **Part (a):** Correct. For $\pi \in (0,1)$, $g(p) = \pi\ln p + (1-\pi)\ln(1-p)$ is strictly concave on $(0,1)$ ($g''(p) = -\pi/p^2 - (1-\pi)/(1-p)^2 < 0$) with unique maximizer $p = \pi$ ($g'(\pi) = 0$). By unimodality of concave functions, its restriction to $\{1,\dots,999\}/1000$ is maximized at one (or both, in a tie) of the grid points adjacent to $\pi$, or at the nearer endpoint $0.001$ or $0.999$ when $\pi \notin [0.001, 0.999]$. Line 171 properly notes that strict propriety holds for expected score, not for nonlinear downstream rewards such as mixture weights or promotion crossings.
- **Part (b):** Correct by construction of step 8a (`self_judged`, `kernel/check.py:79–80`), which compares the resolution's verified `proposer` principal against the forecast's recorded `proposer` principal. Line 169 appropriately notes the performative, collusion, and multi-principal caveats.

### Proposition 3 (value), Remark, and Example (§4.3, lines 183–216)
- **Part (a):** Correct for complete pre-fixed blocks $B_m$ under Assumption (A). Because $p_{k,i}(Y_i), b_i(Y_i) \in [10^{-3}, 1 - 10^{-3}]$, both the convex combination $Q_m(Y_{B_m}) = \sum_k w_{k,m}\prod_{i \in B_m} p_{k,i}(Y_i)$ and the base product $\prod_{i \in B_m} b_i(Y_i)$ lie in $[10^{-3|B_m|}, (1 - 10^{-3})^{|B_m|}]$, so their log ratio divided by $|B_m|\ln 999$ satisfies $\bar D_m \in [-1, 1]$. Under Assumption (A), $\bar D_1, \dots, \bar D_{m-1}$ are $\mathcal G_{m-1}$-measurable, so $\lambda_m \in [0, 1 - \frac{1}{m+1}] \subset [0, 1)$ is predictable and $1 + \lambda_m \bar D_m \ge \frac{1}{m+1} > 0$. Under $H_0^{\mathrm{val}}: \mathbb E[\bar D_m \mid \mathcal G_{m-1}] \le 0$, $\mathbb E[1 + \lambda_m \bar D_m \mid \mathcal G_{m-1}] \le 1$, so $W_t$ is a non-negative supermartingale with $W_0 = 1$ and Ville's inequality gives $\Pr(\sup_t W_t \ge 1/\alpha) \le \alpha$.
- **Part (b):** Correct when weights update sequentially over the same blocks $B_1, \dots, B_t$. With uniform prior $w_{k,1} = 1/K$ and $w_{k,m} \propto \prod_{j < m}\prod_{i \in B_j} p_{k,i}(Y_i)$, the product telescopes: $\prod_{m \le t} Q_m(Y_{B_m}) = \frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^K \prod_{m \le t}\prod_{i \in B_m} p_{k,i}(Y_i) \ge \frac{1}{K}\max_k \prod_{m \le t}\prod_{i \in B_m} p_{k,i}(Y_i)$. Taking logs, subtracting $\sum_{m \le t}\sum_{i \in B_m}\ln b_i(Y_i)$, and using that $b$ is in the roster gives both inequalities on line 204.
- **Tension with partial block resolution (line 183 vs. lines 192–208):** Line 183 states that if some events of $B_m$ do not resolve, $\bar D_m$ is computed on the resolved subset $R_m \subseteq B_m$ and the null is stated for that statistic. In Proposition 3 (lines 192–208), however, the formulas for $Q_m$, $w_{k,m}$, $\bar D_m$, and part (b) are still written over $B_m$ and $|B_m|$ rather than $R_m$ and $|R_m|$. For part (b)'s telescoping identity to hold when some events go unresolved, $B_j$ must be replaced by $R_j$ in $w_{k,m}$, $Q_m$, and line 204.
- **Remark (line 212):** Sound on every point: (1) per-event betting in resolution order conditions later stakes and weights on co-realized outcomes excluded from the forecast-time null; (2) freezing stakes across correlated within-sitting events still fails because a product of correlated factors need not be a supermartingale; (3) conditional independence *plus* eventwise nulls would validate frozen-stake per-event betting; (4) a mixture of product forecasts can beat the base rate's product in joint log score by representing within-sitting dependence even without marginal eventwise skill; and (5) scoring the product of mixture marginals $\prod_{i \in B_m} q_i(Y_i)$ breaks the $\ln K$ regret bound of part (b) when members disagree within a block.
- **Example (line 214):** Mathematically sound and numerically exact. With $b = 1/2$, shared outcome $Y \sim \mathrm{Bern}(1/2)$, and product forecast $q \in \{0.95, 0.05\}^n$ chosen by an independent fair coin, $\mathbb E[\bar D_m \mid \mathcal G_{m-1}] = \frac{\ln(1.9) + \ln(0.1)}{2\ln 999} \approx -0.1202 < 0$ for both the block average and each event. Running `sim_filtration.py` reproduces every figure on line 214: block crossing rate `0.0000` and per-event crossing rate `0.1325` (13%) across 4,000 runs of 300 sittings at $n=20$; `0.0000` and `0.5035` (50%) across 2,000 runs of 200 sittings at $n=50$; and `0.0000` with `--independent`. (Note: the ancillary script also still supports `--roster 20 300 2000`, which gives `0.2850` for the implementation form and `0.0000` for the block form, though as the third referee noted and the Remark now explains, the 3-member roster mixture has positive joint expected score $+0.0850$ at $n=20$ because the mixture models within-block correlation.)

### Proposition 4 (liveness, lines 218–235)
- **Part (a):** Mathematically correct for the process defined on line 220. Since $0 \le s_j \le n_j$, $\hat q_j = (s_j+1)/(n_j+2) \in (0, 1)$, so for $p_0 \in (0,1)$, $\lambda_j = \max(0, (\hat q_j - p_0)/[p_0(1-p_0)]) \in [0, 1/p_0)$. For $Y_j$ (or $\bar Y_j$) $\in [0,1]$, $1 + \lambda_j(Y_j - p_0) \ge 1 - \lambda_j p_0 > 0$, and predictability plus $\mathbb E[Y_j \mid \mathcal F_{j-1}] \le p_0$ yields $\mathbb E[M_j \mid \mathcal F_{j-1}] \le M_{j-1}$. Continuing one product across windows makes each window end a stopping time of a single supermartingale.
  - *Hidden flaw in applying line 220's simultaneous-item mean rule to `judge/status.py`:* Line 218 states that an item discharges a batch of writs, and line 220 states that simultaneous items count as one with mean outcome $\bar Y_j$. In `judge/status.py` (`item_process`, lines 101–135), however, a timely or tainted merge of resolutions is emitted as **one** `"batch"` item ($436$ with $Y=1$, $11$ with $Y=0$ at `seq 7809`), whereas expired forecasts (`expire(e)`, lines 101–106) are emitted as **separate `"expiry"` items ($Y=0$), one per individual expired forecast** ($194$ items, of which $188$ share a timestamp). Applying line 220's simultaneous-item mean rule directly to `judge/status.py`'s items therefore collapses a batch of 6–12 expired forecasts into a single $Y=0$ factor while leaving every timely batch as a single $Y=1$ factor, inflating liveness wealth at `seq 7809` from $1.59 \times 10^9$ (641 items) to $6.19 \times 10^{58}$ (488 timestamps). To be symmetric, either expiries must be grouped by batch/deadline on the same unit as merges, or items must be weighted by writ count.
- **Part (b):** Correct. For $k$ windows with false-crossing probabilities $r_j \le \alpha$, independence gives $1 - \prod_{j=1}^k(1-r_j) \le 1 - (1-\alpha)^k$ (with equality when $r_j = \alpha$) and the union bound gives $\sum_{j=1}^k r_j \le k\alpha$.

### Proposition 5 (conjunctive promotion, lines 236–240)
- Correct. Because promotion requires both $W_t \ge 1/\alpha$ and $M_t \ge 1/\alpha$ at the same prefix (along with deterministic checks), the promotion event is contained in $\{\sup_t W_t \ge 1/\alpha\} \cap \{\sup_t M_t \ge 1/\alpha\}$, which has probability at most $\alpha$ whenever $H_0^{\mathrm{val}}$ or $H_0^{\mathrm{live}}$ holds (Berger's intersection–union principle). Line 240 properly notes that this gives no familywise bound across multiple layers.

### Proposition 6 (replay-bound change, lines 246–265)
- **Parts (a)–(c):** Tautologically sound as conditional statements, and line 250 now candidly discloses the gap between specification and enforcement (`judge/judge.py` checks only that the first post-5564 installing push carries a grant-named `SPEC.md`; CI spot-checks 3 of 706 rows; full replay is a reviewer session). 2. **`Propriety.lean` (`Claim.log_score_proper`, `Claim.log_score_strict`) $\leftrightarrow$ Proposition 2(a):**

3. **`Mixture.lean` (`Claim.prod_Q`, `Claim.regret`, `Claim.regret_base`) $\leftrightarrow$ Proposition 3(b):**

### Methodological validity
Drafts 8–9 fixed the post-hoc random-$L$ threshold (via $\ell(\ell+1)/\alpha$), the 19 post-cutoff events, and the 4 pending sittings at `2026-11-25T11:05:35Z`. However, **`block_on_log.py` still has two bugs in lines 124–143 that make its lag-class processes non-adapted across observation times `as_of`:**

1. **Late resolutions after `deadline` (`resolves_by`) mutate past sittings (`block_on_log.py:124`):**
   Line 338 states: *"A sitting is scored once every member has resolved or passed its deadline."* In `block_on_log.py:124`, however, an event's completion time and outcome are set to `done = ts_of[r["resolution_seq"]] if r else d["deadline"]` and `Y = r["Y"] if r else None` **without requiring `ts_of[r["resolution_seq"]] <= d["deadline"]`**. On `e580b64:log.jsonl`, **168 of the 2,801 resolved member events (across 33 of the 476 sittings) resolved *after* their `resolves_by` deadline**! Worse, **8 sittings are split** between on-time resolutions ($\le \text{deadline}$) and late resolutions ($> \text{deadline}$)—for example, sitting `2026-11-12T21:00:00Z` (deadline `2026-11-13T02:00:00Z`), where 3 events resolved before `02:00:00Z` and 3 events resolved more than 14 hours after the deadline at `2026-11-13T16:13:32Z` (or `2026-11-08T02:00:00Z`, deadline `07:00:00Z`, where 5 events resolved at `03:13Z` and the 6th at `07:20:23Z`).
   If `block_on_log.py` is run at any observation time `as_of` between `deadline` and the late resolution (e.g., `2026-11-13T03:00:00Z`), the sitting has already passed its deadline, so `block_on_log.py` marks it "done" at `02:00:00Z` and scores it on only the 3 on-time events; once the late resolutions arrive at `16:13:32Z`, `block_on_log.py` **retroactively changes** `done` from `02:00:00Z` to `16:13:32Z`, changes the scored `block` from 3 events to 6 events, changes $\bar D_m$, and shifts downstream lag-class assignments and stakes. Even the 2 unresolved events on 24 November (`seqs 7424–7425`, deadlines `08:00Z` and `09:00Z`) could still be resolved by a future log entry under `SPEC §8` and retroactively alter the 24 November sittings.

2. **Deleting `pending` sittings before lag-class assignment mutates class membership (`block_on_log.py:128–143`):**
   Lines 128–129 delete `pending` sittings (`max(e["done"]) > T_obs`) *before* the greedy lag-class assignment loop at lines 135–143. Because the log contains **98 inversions** where sitting $c_1 < c_2$ finishes after $c_2$ ($\text{done}_1 > \text{done}_2$), deleting `pending` sittings before assigning classes means that at an intermediate observation time $\text{done}_2 \le T_{\text{obs}} < \text{done}_1$, sitting $c_1$ is deleted as pending and does not occupy a lag class when $c_2$ is assigned; after $\text{done}_1$, $c_1$ is no longer pending, takes that lag class at $c_1$, and retroactively bumps $c_2$ into a different lag class! Concretely, running `block_on_log.py --rev e580b64 2026-11-03T13:30:00Z` assigns sitting `2026-11-03T12:00:00Z` (done at `13:12:41Z`) to **lag class 0** (while `2026-11-03T11:00:00Z`, done at `14:23:27Z`, is pending), whereas running `block_on_log.py --rev e580b64 2026-11-03T15:00:00Z` retroactively moves `2026-11-03T12:00:00Z` to **lag class 1**.

*How to fix both and what happens to the numbers:* Count a resolution only if `ts_of[r["resolution_seq"]] <= d["deadline"]` (so every event's status—resolved on time or expired at deadline—is permanently fixed by `d["deadline"]`, and `done <= deadline`), and assign lag classes without allowing unresolved earlier sittings to be skipped.
